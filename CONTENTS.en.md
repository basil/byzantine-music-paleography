## Description

**Abstract:** The textbook is a comprehensive introduction to the historical
development of Byzantine musical notation. The work begins with an examination
of Greek paleography and codicology, analyzing writing materials, the form of
manuscripts (from scrolls to codices), and types of Greek writing (capital
letters, small letters). The treatise then studies the types of musical
notation. It presents phonetic notation (9th-15th centuries) for the melodic
reading of biblical texts, as well as various primitive local notations. This
is followed by Paleo-Byzantine notation (10th-12th centuries), the first major
system, which was intervallic (it did not specify intervals precisely) and is
divided into the Athonite (Chartres) and Agiopolite (Coislin) families. The
main part of the book is devoted to Middle Byzantine notation (12th-19th
centuries), also known as the "Old System". This notation is intervallic,
clearly representing melodic movements. Its theoretical foundations, such as
the Octoechos, and the didactic triptych of metrophony-variation-melody are
examined. Particular emphasis is given to the art of gesture, the Great Signs,
and the process of musical explanation--the decoding of shorthand notation,
which led to the New Method of 1814.
